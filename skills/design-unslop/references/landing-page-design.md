# Landing Page Design

A landing page has one job: turn a visitor into a user, a signup, or a sale. Everything on it serves that job or it goes.

## The structure (top to bottom)
| Section | What it must do |
|---|---|
| Nav | Logo + one CTA. No clutter, no dead links. |
| Hero | One value proposition in one sentence (what it is + who it's for + why it's better), one primary CTA, one visual that shows the product doing its thing. No carousel, no stock photo of people pointing at a laptop. |
| Social proof | Real numbers, real logos, real quotes with real names. "Trusted by 10,000 teams" only if it's true and verifiable. |
| Benefits | 3–5 benefits, not features. "Saves 2 hours a day" beats "has an API". Each benefit gets one line + one visual or number. |
| How it works | 3 steps, no more. Each step is one sentence. |
| Product proof | A real screenshot, a real demo, or a real result. Not a mockup of a mockup. |
| Pricing | Clear, no hidden tiers, no "contact us" as the only option unless it really is enterprise-only. |
| FAQ | The 5 questions visitors actually ask, answered in one or two sentences each. |
| Final CTA | Same CTA as the hero, repeated. No new pitch. |
| Footer | Real links, real contact, real legal pages. |

## Rules
- **One action per screen.** The hero asks for one thing. The page asks for one thing. If the page asks for three things, the visitor does none of them.
- **Benefits, not features.** A feature is what it does. A benefit is what the visitor gets. Lead with the benefit, support with the feature.
- **Real proof, not decoration.** Every number, logo, and quote is real and checkable. A fake testimonial is worse than no testimonial.
- **Fast.** The hero renders in under 2 seconds on a mid-range phone. If it doesn't, cut the heaviest thing first.
- **Mobile-first.** Most visitors arrive on a phone. Design the mobile layout first; the desktop layout is the wide version of it.
- **No dark patterns.** No fake urgency ("only 2 left!" when it's not true), no hidden unsubscribe, no pre-ticked boxes. Trust converts better than tricks.
- **Copy that a person would say.** Read the hero line out loud. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.

## The 5-second test
Show the page to someone for 5 seconds, then hide it. They must be able to answer: what is this, who is it for, and what do I do next? If they can't, the hero failed — rewrite it before touching anything else.

## Audit checks
| # | Check | Fail means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero states what it is + who it's for + one CTA in 5 seconds | Visitor can't answer the 3 questions |
| 2 | Every number, logo, and quote is real and verifiable | A fake or unverifiable claim |
| 3 | One primary action per screen | Two or more competing CTAs in the hero |
| 4 | Page loads fast on a mid-range phone | Hero takes over 2 seconds |
| 5 | Mobile layout designed first, works at 360px | Desktop-only layout that breaks on mobile |
| 6 | No dark patterns | Fake urgency, hidden opt-outs, pre-ticked boxes |
