# Salary negotiation — market data, three numbers, one counter

Distilled from `salary-negotiator`. Own words throughout.

## When to use
Evaluating a written offer, answering "what are your salary expectations?",
juggling multiple offers, negotiating a raise internally, handling an
exploding offer or deadline.

## Core concepts
- **Never give the first number if you can avoid it.** "What's your expected
  salary?" → "I'd like to understand the full scope first — what's the
  budgeted range for this role?" Whoever anchors first sets the frame.
- **Negotiate the total package.** Base, bonus, equity/RSUs, sign-on,
  relocation, remote flexibility, start date, title, review timeline. A "no"
  on base often becomes a "yes" on sign-on or equity.
- **Market data is the leverage.** Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Pave, peer
  conversations — come with a range, not a feeling. Negotiate against the top
  of the band, not the median, and quote the band as a range for that role in
  that market — never a single fixed figure presented as the market rate.

## Workflow
1. **Research.** Gather the market range for the role, level, and market from
   at least two sources. Note the band, not one number.
2. **Set your numbers — three figures, kept private:** ideal (the ask),
   target (happy to sign), walk-away (decline). The walk-away is never
   revealed to the employer; it is a discipline tool.
3. **Deflect early salary questions.** Use the range-request script. If
   forced, give a wide, research-backed range with the top at your ideal —
   never a single number, never your current salary (it anchors you to your
   past, and is often illegal to ask for).
4. **Evaluate the written offer.** Score each component against your numbers.
   Find the 2–3 levers with the most upside (usually base + sign-on + equity).
5. **Make the counter.** One consolidated ask, framed collaboratively: "I'm
   excited about the role and the team. Based on my research and the scope,
   I'd be able to sign at [X base, Y sign-on]. Can we get there?" Then
   silence. Do not negotiate against yourself.
6. **Handle pushback.** Expect "this is our best offer" (often not true),
   "the band doesn't allow it" (ask about sign-on/equity, which often sit
   outside bands), or split-the-difference. Answer each calmly, one round at
   a time — 2–3 rounds is normal.
7. **Close cleanly.** Once terms are agreed, confirm in writing, thank
   everyone, and stop negotiating. Post-acceptance renegotiation burns bridges.

## Common pitfalls
- Accepting the first offer. A polite counter almost never rescinds an offer.
- Negotiating against yourself ("maybe around X, but I'm flexible...").
- Revealing current salary. Deflect to the market rate for the role.
- Only negotiating base. Sign-on, equity refreshers, and remote stipends are
  real money left on the table.
- **Using a fake competing offer.** Lies get discovered and destroy trust.
  Real leverage only — even "I'm in late stages elsewhere" must be true.
- Exploding-offer panic. "Sign by Friday or it's gone" is usually negotiable:
  ask for a few more days to decide thoughtfully.
