# Freelance practice — positioning, pricing, pipeline, contracts

Distilled from `freelance-guide`. Own words throughout.

## Core concepts
- **Positioning beats prospecting.** "Designer for SaaS onboarding flows"
  gets hired; "freelance designer" gets ignored. A sharp niche makes
  marketing easier and commands higher rates.
- **Pricing models.** Hourly (simple, caps income), project/fixed (rewards
  efficiency, needs good scoping), retainer (stable, best for ongoing work),
  value-based (highest upside, hardest to sell). Most successful freelancers
  migrate toward fixed/retainer over time.
- **The rate formula.** Target annual income ÷ billable hours (not 2080 —
  use 1000–1200 after admin, marketing, vacation) × 1.3 (taxes, benefits,
  dry spells). Then sanity-check against market rates for the niche.
- **Pipeline is survival.** The feast-famine cycle kills freelancers who stop
  marketing when busy. Rule: always have 2–3 warm prospects, even at full
  capacity. Marketing is a weekly habit, not a panic response.
- **Contracts before work.** Scope, deliverables, timeline, payment terms,
  revision rounds, kill fee, IP transfer on final payment. A one-page
  agreement prevents most disputes. Never start without a signature and
  (ideally) a deposit. See job-search `contracts-proposals.md` for clauses.
- **Cash-flow discipline.** Separate business account, quarterly tax set-asides
  (roughly a quarter to a third of income, depending on jurisdiction), 3–6
  month buffer. Late payments are normal — the buffer is what makes them
  survivable.

## Workflow
1. **Validate the leap.** Before quitting: land 1–2 paying clients or save
   6 months of expenses. Define the niche and ideal client profile in one
   paragraph each.
2. **Set up the basics.** Business registration/tax status for the
   jurisdiction, separate bank account, simple invoicing tool, one-page
   contract template, work-samples site.
3. **Price it.** Run the rate formula, research 5–10 competitors in the
   niche, pick a starting rate. Plan to raise it every 3–6 months as demand
   proves out.
4. **Get the first 3 clients.** Warm network first (announce the launch
   properly — most freelancers under-announce), then targeted outreach,
   freelance platforms as a supplement (not the strategy), agency
   partnerships, and content in the niche.
5. **Run the engagement.** Kickoff call → written proposal with scope and
   price → 30–50% deposit → weekly updates → delivery → final invoice
   (net-15 or net-30 terms, late fee stated). Over-communicate; silence
   breeds anxiety.
6. **Manage scope creep.** "Happy to do that — it's outside our current
   scope, so here's what it would add in time and cost." Friendly, immediate,
   in writing. Creep handled late becomes resentment.
7. **Build the flywheel.** Every engagement ends with: testimonial request,
   referral ask, case-study permission, and a check-in scheduled for 90 days
   out. Past clients are the cheapest new business.

## Common pitfalls
- Underpricing to "get started" — low rates attract price-sensitive clients
  who are the hardest to serve and never refer premium work.
- No contract, no deposit.
- Saying yes to everything. Red flags: hard haggling on price, vague scope,
  "exposure" offers, urgency without respect.
- Stopping marketing when busy — the pipeline gap hits 4–8 weeks later.
- Blurring work/life boundaries; ignoring taxes and admin.
