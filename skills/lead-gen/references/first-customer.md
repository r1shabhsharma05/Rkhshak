# First-customer finding — public signals, qualified shortlists

Distilled from `first-customer-finder` (MIT). Own words throughout.

## Workflow
1. **Understand the product.** What it does, who it is for, the pain it
   removes, and — critically — what it cannot do. A prospect that needs a
   missing capability is excluded at this step.
2. **Build a public-signal search plan.** Multiple query angles and source
   types: public discussions, GitHub issues, forums, reviews, company pages.
   Look for manual workflows, repeated workaround complaints, migration or
   churn signals, competitor frustration, and timing signals (hiring,
   launching, expanding, adopting a relevant workflow).
3. **Research safely.** Public, intentionally shared professional or business
   information only. Open original pages; record publication date separately
   from the date checked; attribute the signal to its actual author/company.
   If a source is blocked or only a snippet is available, keep it outside the
   qualified shortlist and explain the coverage gap. Do not imply every
   platform was searched.
4. **Qualify and deduplicate.** Score each prospect on five axes:
   - pain strength, product fit, timing, public reachability, evidence quality.
   Remove duplicates and weak matches. Use a verified company homepage or
   public professional profile as the entity URL — never a name alone or a
   platform homepage. One entity may carry several signals. A prospect without
   a cited pain, need, or timing signal is speculative-fit only and must not
   appear in the primary shortlist.
   - Check whether the problem was already resolved, the author is selling
     rather than buying, the product lacks a required capability, or the buyer
     is outside the requested profile. Put material counter-evidence in the
     caution or exclude the candidate. A high numerical score cannot override
     contradictory evidence. Never fill a requested quota with weak matches.
5. **Draft outreach, never send.** Identify the target role/function and label
   it observed or inferred. Do not invent a named decision-maker. Verify a
   concrete official/public contact route (relevant public thread, published
   business contact page, public professional profile), record its URL and why
   it suits. A visible route is not permission to promote there — inspect
   community rules first. If no appropriate route is found, say so and give a
   manual research next step, not a guessed address.
   - One short opener in the buyer's problem language, with a specific
     low-friction CTA that can be accepted, declined, or forwarded. Offer a
     realistic next step (workflow review, checklist, demo). Do not claim an
     asset, integration, customer result, or capability that is unverified.
   - Never pretend to know the person, overstate familiarity, or mention
     unrelated personal details.
6. **Produce the report.** Lead with the most actionable evidence, in this
   order: verdict → ICP (buyer, job, trigger, disqualifiers) → top prospect
   → prospect shortlist (stable ID, source/date, score, why now, target role,
   verified contact route or missing route, concrete next step, CTA, opener,
   caution) → repeated patterns → seven-day manual outreach plan (low-volume
   validation sequence) → limits (missing evidence, what real conversations
   must confirm). Markdown report + CSV by default.
7. **Refine when the user responds.** Take short feedback by prospect ID
   ("P1 and P3 are a fit; avoid enterprise buyers") and re-run the search.
   Do not delay the first report waiting for feedback.
