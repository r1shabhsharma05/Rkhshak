# Engagement — comments, replies, DMs

## Comments on other people's posts
Commenting is the highest-leverage twenty minutes on Instagram and the easiest to do badly. A comment near the top of a reel with 40,000 views is seen by more people than most accounts' own posts — and it is the one place a stranger can tap straight through to a profile. A generic comment is worse than none: it costs a tap that goes nowhere and marks the account as an engagement-pod account to the creator.

**Input**: the user pastes the post or reel text (or a screenshot) with the account name. If they give a URL that cannot be opened, ask them to paste it. Do not scrape the feed and do not post anything.

**The nine comment types** — pick by what the post actually is; never default to type 1:
| # | Type | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add a datum | The post makes a claim you can support with a number | "Same for us: 40% of…" |
| 2 | Add the missing case | The post is right but incomplete | "This holds until {condition}." |
| 3 | Respectful disagree | You genuinely think it is wrong | Name the agreement first, then the fork |
| 4 | Extend one line | One line in it is the good one | Quote it, build on it |
| 5 | Ask the real question | The post skipped the hard part | One question, specific |
| 6 | The receipt | You have done the thing they described | What happened, two sentences |
| 7 | The correction | There is a factual error | Be right, be brief, be kind, be sure |
| 8 | The reframe | Right facts, wrong frame | "Another way to read this:" |
| 9 | The one-liner | The post needs nothing, you want presence | Under 10 words, funny or true |

**Rules**: 1–3 sentences (a paragraph collapses behind "more" and nobody taps it). Never open with "Great post", "Love this", "So true", "This 👏", "Needed this today", or the creator's first name with an exclamation mark — all invisible. No emoji-only comments, no emoji as the first character. Never restate the reel. One idea — two points reads as a hijack. Say the specific thing: if the comment could sit under any post on the topic, it is noise. No pitching, ever — not the offer, not the link, not "check out my page". Early matters more here than anywhere: a comment in the first hour on a reel that then travels gets carried with it.

**Output**: two options of different types, labelled, plus one line on which to post and why. Run both through the humaniser first. **Batch mode**: 5–10 posts as pasted text in one message, one comment each in a single block; keep a note of who was commented on this week — commenting on the same three accounts every day is visible.

**Never**: do not auto-post, do not automate comments, do not publish on the user's behalf. Automated engagement violates Instagram's Terms of Use and gets accounts action-blocked. This skill writes the comment. The user posts it.

## Replies under the user's own posts
The comment thread under your own post is where reach is decided: every reply is another interaction, replies in the first hour do most of the work, and a reply can also be a Reel — the single most underused move on the platform.

**Triage first** — sort every comment into one of six buckets and say the counts out loud:
| Bucket | What it is | What it gets |
|---|---|---|
| KEYWORD | The word you asked them to comment | The promised thing, sent by hand or by the approved tool |
| LEAD | Someone describing the problem you solve | A real answer in public, then a door |
| SUBSTANCE | Adds data, disagrees, extends | The longest reply on the thread |
| QUESTION | A question a lot of people have | This one becomes a Reel, not just a reply |
| SUPPORT | "🔥", "great post", a tag | A like, and 3–8 words at most |
| NOISE | Pitch, spam, bad faith, bait | Nothing, or one line and out |

Write in that order and stop when the value stops.

**How to reply**: answer the actual question — if someone asks how, tell them how in the reply; do not send them to the DMs for an answer they could have had. Use their name once, at the start, without an exclamation mark. Match their length. To a critic: concede the true part first, in their words, then hold the line; never delete, never get defensive, never reply twice on the same thread. To a hater: nothing — a reply is reach, and reach is what they came for; hide the comment if it is abusive. To a lead: answer fully in public; the door is one sentence at the end, an offer of help, not a pitch.

**The move most people miss**: if a question in the comments is one thirty other people also have, **reply to it with a Reel**. Instagram attaches the comment to the new video as a sticker, the asker gets notified, and a question with real demand behind it becomes a post with the hook already written. Flag every qualifying QUESTION and hand it to the Reel workflow as formula #16.

**Keyword comments**: if the post used a keyword ask, those comments are the whole point. Reply to each, then send what was promised. If the user has automation through Instagram's own tools or an approved partner, let it run; if not, the replies are manual. Never bulk-DM people who did not comment. Nothing is posted until the user says yes.

## DMs
DMs are the only place on the platform where money actually changes hands — and where most accounts burn the goodwill their content earned. The difference is entirely about who moved first.

**The three kinds of DM, and only three are worth writing**:
1. **Reply to a hand raised.** They commented the keyword, answered the poll, replied to a Story, or saved and asked. They moved first. ~90% of the DMs worth sending.
2. **Warm approach.** Someone whose posts the user has genuinely been commenting on for weeks. A shared thread already exists.
3. **Collab or brand pitch.** A specific proposal to a specific account, with a reason it is them.

Everything else is cold DMing strangers — the lowest-yield use of the same hour. Say so plainly, offer the alternative (comment on those ten accounts for two weeks first), then write it if they still want it.

**Before writing, get the specifics** (one batched question): who (handle, what they do, what started this); the trigger (the actual reason to message today — a keyword, a Story reply, a post — not "they fit the ICP"); what the user wants (a conversation, a sale, a collab, a referral). If there is no trigger, there is no message. Say so.

**Keyword delivery**: they commented one word; they are expecting the thing.
```
{their name}, here it is: {the thing, or the link}.

{one line on how to use it}

{one question they can answer in four words}
```
Send the thing **first**, in message one, with no gate. A keyword post that replies "before I send it, can I ask what you do?" is a bait and switch and it is remembered. Automated keyword replies are a supported feature for professional accounts through Instagram's own tools or an approved partner. Sending unsolicited bulk DMs is not, and it is the fastest route to a restricted account.

**First message to someone warm**: 2–4 sentences (a screen of text is a delete); reference the specific thing, in their words; give before asking (a number, a template, a name, an answer); one small ask ("Worth a quick call?" beats a walkthrough); no link and no calendar in message one; no voice note to a stranger.

**Collab pitch**: four lines in order — what you have watched them do, the specific idea, what they get, what you need from them. A collab post lands on both grids and reaches both audiences — the strongest single growth mechanic that does not involve paying anybody. Be concrete about who does what.

**Follow-ups — two, that is the number**: +4 days, adding something new (never "just bumping this" — if there is nothing new, there is no follow-up); +10 days, the close-the-loop message (say you will stop, and mean it — this one gets a surprising share of total replies, because it removes the pressure). Then stop. A third converts nobody and costs the relationship.

**Never**: never automate outreach DMs; never use a tool that sends on a schedule to people who did not interact; never fabricate having watched something, a mutual, or a shared anything; never open with "Hey! Quick question" and then not ask a question; never send the pitch in the same message as the compliment.

**Output**: the message, the character count, and the two follow-ups with the day each goes out, all humanised. The user sends every one by hand, or through their own approved automation for keyword replies only.
