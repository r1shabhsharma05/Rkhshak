# Shape the Idea — before any plan exists

Distilled from three sources, in our own words: brainstorming (MIT), idea-refine (MIT), thinking-out-loud. Full method lives in those sources. Use when an idea is still vague, when a ramble arrives instead of a brief, or when assumptions need stress-testing before planning.

## Brainstorming
- **First: what do you actually know?** State the knowns before generating ideas. An idea built on an unknown is a guess with decoration.
- **The conversation.** Ask one question at a time; listen to the answer before asking the next. A brainstorm is a conversation, not a questionnaire.
- **Play it back.** Restate the idea in your own words and let the user correct it. The playback is where misunderstandings surface — cheaply.
- **Size the work.** Before writing anything down, name the rough size: an afternoon, a week, a quarter. A mis-sized idea plans wrong from the start.
- **The written design.** Only after the conversation: a short design doc (problem, direction, non-goals, rough size). The doc is the output, not the conversation.

## Idea refinement (diverge, then converge)
1. **Understand and expand (divergent).** Grow the idea: angles, users, variants, wild options. No judging in this phase.
2. **Evaluate and converge.** Score the options against the actual constraints (time, skill, cost, risk). Kill the ones that fail loudly; say why.
3. **Sharpen and ship.** The surviving direction, written as: problem statement, recommended direction, key assumptions to validate, MVP scope, what is explicitly NOT being done (and why), open questions.
- **Anti-patterns:** refining forever (set a deadline for the decision); falling in love with the first option; an MVP that contains everything.

## Thinking out loud (the echo contract)
When a long, messy, stream-of-consciousness ramble arrives — usually voice dictation — act on nothing until the echo brief is approved:
- **The echo.** Play back what was heard, structured: the asks, the decisions, the open questions. Short, in the user's words where possible.
- **The interview (optional).** Only if the echo leaves genuine gaps: one question at a time, each with a default.
- **Capture mode.** For multi-message rambles: collect first, echo once, never act on a partial ramble.
- **Persistence.** The approved echo becomes the brief the plan is built from. An unapproved echo is not a brief.
- **When not to use:** the message is already a clear instruction — echoing it back is noise, not diligence.
