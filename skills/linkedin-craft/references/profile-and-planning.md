# Profile optimiser + content planner — pillars, founder angles, weekly plans

Distilled from `linkedin-skills` (MIT bundle: profile-optimizer,
content-planner, founder layer). Own words; reference files distilled,
not copied.

## Profile optimiser
- **Audit first.** Read the profile as a recruiter or reader would: headline,
  about, experience, featured. List what is unclear, unsupported, or missing
  — before rewriting a word.
- **Rewrite from evidenced facts only.** Real role, real skills, real project,
  real outcome. Every number traces to something the user stated; every
  unknown is `[N]`. An invented claim in a profile is worse than in a post —
  it sits there for every future reader.
- **Headline rule:** who you help + how, in one line. No title salad, no
  invented employers, no inflated user counts.
- **About rule:** the problem you solve, proof in one line, what to do next
  (contact route the user actually monitors).
- Return the rewrite as copy-paste text. The owner applies it himself.

## Content planner (a week, not a wish)
1. **Ask: founder plan or general plan?** The pillar set swaps.
   - General pillars: build in public, teach, opinion, proof.
   - Founder pillars: Conviction / Building in public / The math / Proof.
2. **One idea per day, one post per idea.** Each day gets a hook formula, the
   fact it rests on (story bank or `[N]`), and the audience it serves.
3. **Mix the formats.** A week of identical list posts is a week of one post
   repeated. Vary story, teaching, opinion, and proof — and vary the hook
   formula with them.
4. **Plan is a plan.** Drafts are still drafted one at a time, audited one at
   a time, and posted by the owner. A plan never posts itself.

## Founder angles (trust with a narrow audience, not broad reach)
Ten fill-in angles, each mapping to a primary goal and a hook formula:
reprice the category; content-to-pipeline; audience of one; the scarce-shots
math; the unglamorous bet; the limit of delegation; designed serendipity;
the evasive-sentence test; the delegation line; the learning gate.
- Pick the angle that matches the goal (investors, hires, design partners —
  a different angle each). Say why it fits that narrow audience.
- Pair with a structural formula (controlled A/B anecdote, false-binary
  dissolve, anecdote-meets-evidence bridge, diverging-curves close). The
  formula shapes the post's logic, not its topic.
- Founder angles compound trust with a narrow, high-value audience instead
  of chasing broad reach. A founder post that chases virality has picked the
  wrong goal.
