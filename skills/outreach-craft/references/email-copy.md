# Cold-email copy — short, specific, one ask

## First email (the shape that survived real use)
- **Under 120 words.** Plain text, no images, no attachments.
- **Subject:** the lead's name or their problem — e.g. "[Business] — one
  observation". Never clever for its own sake; never clickbait.
- **Structure (4 parts, in order):**
  1. One true, researched sentence about them — the observation. It must
     be checkable from a public source (a missing website, a recent
     review, a hiring post, a public statement). If it cannot be checked,
     it does not go in.
  2. One sentence on what you do, in their problem language — not your
     feature list.
  3. One concrete proof or next step — a demo link, a concept, a specific
     offer. One link at most.
  4. One small ask — a 10-minute call — with a graceful out ("If not, no
     worries at all").
- **Never in email one:** price, a long pitch, multiple asks, attachments,
  more than one link, or a claim you cannot prove.

## Personalisation (real research only)
- One named fact per message, drawn from research on that lead: a missing
  website, a recent review quote, a dated public post, a stated email
  address. Quote the source in the log, not in the email.
- Flattery is not personalisation. "I love what you're building" with no
  fact is filler — cut it.
- Never pretend to know the person, overstate familiarity, or mention
  unrelated personal details.
- Never invent a named decision-maker. If no named person could be
  verified, say so and route to a published contact instead.

## What the copy must never contain
- Fake numbers, fake testimonials, fake customers, fake results.
- A claimed integration, asset, or capability that is unverified.
- "Join thousands of users" or any social proof without a real source.
- A promise about timing, price, or outcome you cannot keep.

## Subject lines (tested, not clever)
- Test subject lines the same way as body copy: one variable at a time,
  logged per contact. A subject that gets opens but no replies is not a
  win — the reply is the metric.
- Keep it under ~50 characters, specific to the lead, no shouting, no
  fake "Re:" / "Fwd:", no false urgency.

## A/B discipline (one variable at a time)
- One version in the field at a time. Changing the subject AND the ask
  AND the opener in one rewrite teaches nothing.
- Every contact is logged with the version they got (e.g. "Version B —
  advice-first"). Never mix versions silently in one batch.
- A version earns its keep only on replies, not on sends. A rewrite after
  a trigger changes exactly one variable; the diagnosis names which one
  and why (see `diagnose-zero-replies.md`).
- Keep the old version and its numbers. A new version that does worse
  must be visible, not quietly replaced.

## Two stream notes
- **Stream A (named person):** advice-first works better than pitch-first
  — lead with a useful observation about their work, not with yourself.
  The ask is a short conversation, not a sale.
- **Stream B (local business):** the observation is usually the missing
  website itself, stated plainly with one nearby-competitor contrast.
  Keep the peer tone — never salesy, never pushy.
